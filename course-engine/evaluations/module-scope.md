# Module scope review

The 0.1.1 protocol allowed several coherent blocks inside an unrestricted module. A learner could
therefore receive a useful but daunting chapter that bundled orientation, a worked system trace,
and changed-input analysis. A stopping sentence between blocks did not give those outcomes their
own completion or retrieval scope.

Engine 0.1.2 defaults to one main outcome, 30–60 minutes of total work and roughly 600–1,200 words
of required prose. These are product defaults to test with learners, not measured attention limits.
The QA script warns above 1,500 approximate prose words or one estimated hour. It excludes fenced
examples and reference destinations from the count, leaves existing courses valid, and does not
claim that short text or passing checks proves good teaching. Languages without whitespace word
boundaries need human review; code and visual exploration still count toward the total effort.

## Worked scope decisions

- A beginner's orientation, complete data trace and failure analysis become three modules. Each
  has its own exercise, explanation and focused retrieval questions. Keep enough context in each
  lesson to understand the task, and share references without repeating the entire chapter.
- A single integration exercise genuinely requiring 90 minutes can remain one module when the
  arc explains the reason and a resumable stopping point. The scope warning remains advisory.
- A learner with four available hours per day can do several modules with breaks and due recall.
  A learner with twenty-minute sessions needs smaller outcomes or a clearly resumable exercise.
  Neither estimate establishes a deadline for mastering the outcome.

## Evidence and limits

Spacing and retrieval research supports repeated opportunities to retrieve with feedback, rather
than judging learning by exposure to explanations. It does not establish this product's word or
minute thresholds. See Carpenter, Pan and Butler, [The science of effective learning with spacing
and retrieval practice](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44159-022-00089-1), 2022.

The scope tests verify warning boundaries and exclusion of fenced source material. They do not
measure model compliance, retention or the quality of a generated course. Follow learner time,
independent explanations, changed-example attempts and due recall to revise these defaults.
